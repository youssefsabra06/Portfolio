Project 01

Solar-powered boat for the Amazon

A solar-electric version of the rabeta, the long-shaft boat used on the rivers around Belém in Brazil. We designed the full-scale drive and charging system, then built a 1:10 prototype of the motor and shaft to prove the control system worked.

Solar Boat, Electro-Mechanical Engineering Practice

My role

  • Led the team of 6 from brief to final design, and wrote the specification, modelling and final-design sections of the report.
  • Modelled the drive: drag and shaft torque, and battery sizing (48 V, 160 Ah for a 26 km round trip).
  • Designed the 22 V to 12 V buck converter, including the 272 µH inductor and its core air gap.
  • With the team, built the 1:10 BLDC prototype on a TI F28379D with Hall-sensor commutation, tuned the PWM duty cycle to hit 1,800 rpm, and added a gearbox that doubled torque from 0.19 to 0.38 Nm.
  • Checked the full-scale shaft structure with FEA in Fusion 360.

Project 02

Power conversion, magnetics and machines

A full simulation study of an electrical energy chain, from AC transmission and power-factor correction, through rectifiers and PWM inverters, to an electromagnetic actuator and DC machines. I modelled each stage in Simulink, MATLAB and FEMM and checked every result against hand calculation.

Unit
Electrical Energy Conversion and Supply
Type
Individual
Date
2025 – 26
Tools
Simulink, MATLAB, FEMM

What I did

  • AC systems: modelled single- and three-phase supplies with transformers, sized a capacitor to correct a 150 kW load from 0.75 to 0.95 power factor, and solved an unbalanced three-phase load for its neutral current.
  • Power electronics: built a bridge rectifier with DC-link filtering, then half-bridge, full-bridge and three-phase PWM inverters with IGBTs, and used FFT analysis to trade switching frequency against harmonic distortion.
  • Magnetics: refined a FEMM model of an electromagnetic actuator, built the matching reluctance circuit, and compared inductance, co-energy and force from both methods, linear and saturating.
  • Machines: built dynamic Simulink models of permanent-magnet and wound-field DC machines, then derived the torque-speed envelope and efficiency curve of a 1.5 MW machine with field weakening.

Caravan tow load device

A tow bar that measures the forces between a car and a caravan while it drives: horizontal pull from a strain gauge, vertical nose load from a piezoelectric sensor, logged by a Raspberry Pi Pico at up to 120 km/h.

My role

  • Led the team of 5 from specification to final design, assigning weekly tasks through the action log.
  • Co-wrote the product design specification and researched sensing concepts, including strain gauges and the piezoelectric and strain gauge combination that won the design matrix.
  • Built the CAD model of the winning concept and refined it over several weeks, including adding a bearing so the strain gauge reads only the horizontal load.
  • Selected the double-row thrust ball bearing, and worked on material selection against the calculated forces.
  • Contributed to the design analysis behind the sensing chain.

Project 05

Vibration-based stiffness testing

Could a piece of metal found at a disaster site stand in for a broken ambulance leaf spring? I measured its Young's modulus from how fast it vibrates, using a microphone circuit, a Raspberry Pi Pico and some Python.

Project
Dynamics lab, material stiffness
Type
Individual
Date
2025
Hardware
Raspberry Pi Pico
Software
Python, JupyterLab

What I did

  • Built the sensing circuit: condenser microphone, high-pass filter, amplifier and Schottky protection feeding the Pico's ADC.
  • Clamped a 300 mm ruler as a cantilever and recorded its vibration at three overhang lengths, three trials each.
  • Wrote a Python pipeline that trims each recording, applies a Butterworth filter and counts zero crossings to find the natural frequency.

Outcome

The material came out stiffer than spring steel, so it would not absorb shocks and is not a safe replacement. The report also traces the scatter in the results back to the setup, mainly clamping and microphone calibration, and suggests how to fix both.

Project 06

Heatsink thermal analysis & LED efficiency

How well does a finned aluminium heatsink cool a high-power LED? We built a parametric thermal model in MATLAB, tested the real heatsink in a 3D-printed fan duct, and compared the two.

Project
Heatsink and LED efficiency characterisation
Type
Group project
Date
2025
Tools
MATLAB, Fusion 360
Data logging
TracerDAQ, 8 ch.

The project

My part: running the TracerDAQ data logging in both labs, assembling the duct and heatsink model in Fusion 360, and writing the introduction and experimental method.

  • Built a parametric thermal model of the heatsink in MATLAB from published fin-array correlations.
  • Designed and 3D-printed a fan duct in Fusion 360 to push even airflow across the fins.
  • Logged eight temperature channels with TracerDAQ for 30, 40 and 50 mm fins, then compared measured and modelled thermal resistance.
  • Used the heatsink results to estimate the efficiency of the high-power LED.

Results

Fin heightMeasured RsinkModel Rsink
30 mm0.32 K/W0.44 K/W
40 mm0.33 K/W0.35 K/W
50 mm0.33 K/W0.30 K/W

At 50 mm the measurement is within 10% of the model. The model predicts resistance falling with fin height, but the measurements stayed nearly flat, which the report puts down to non-uniform airflow from the duct and heat lost through the fin tips. LED efficiency came out at about 48%.
